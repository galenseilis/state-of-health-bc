Indicator: Mortality rates, British Columbia

Status: first published indicator (milestone M3 in progress). Regenerate with PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m sohbc build mortality_rates_bc.

This is the project’s first end-to-end indicator: it is fetched from a catalogued source, tidied by the pipeline, and published as a versioned dataset plus the views below. Each indicator page states its provenance and caveats (see the roadmap, milestone M3).

Provenance

Field Value
Domain Mortality
Definition Deaths and the mortality rate (deaths per 1,000 population), all ages, both sexes, resident in British Columbia; crude and directly age-standardised (WHO World Standard Population 2000–2025) (Ahmad et al. 2001)
Source Statistics Canada, table 13-10-0710-01, Mortality rates, by age group (Statistics Canada, n.d.)
Access Statistics Canada Web Data Service (automated full-table download)
Form indicator table (published summary statistics)
Geography British Columbia, place of residence
Coverage 1991–present (annual); observation window shown on each figure
Frequency / refresh annual
Licence Statistics Canada Open Licence
Proxy note A proxy for the mortality domain, not the state of health itself (why)

Trend

rows = FileAttachment("../data/published/mortality_rates_bc.csv").csv({typed: true})
rates = rows.filter(d => d.characteristic === "Mortality rate per 1,000 population" && d.age_group === "Age at time of death, all ages")
counts = rows.filter(d => d.characteristic === "Number of deaths")
asr = FileAttachment("../data/published/mortality_rates_bc_age_standardised.csv").csv({typed: true})
trend = FileAttachment("../data/published/mortality_rates_bc_trend.json").json()
series = rates.map(d => ({year: d.ref_date, rate: d.value, series: "Crude"}))
  .concat(asr.map(d => ({year: d.ref_date, rate: d.value, series: "Age-standardised"})))
Plot.plot({
  title: "Mortality rate, British Columbia (per 1,000 population)",
  x: {label: "Year", tickFormat: "d"},
  y: {grid: true, label: "Deaths per 1,000 population"},
  color: {legend: true},
  marks: [
    Plot.rectX([{x1: 2020, x2: 2025}], {x1: "x1", x2: "x2", fill: "grey", fillOpacity: 0.12}),
    Plot.ruleX([trend.change_point], {stroke: "red", strokeDasharray: "4,4"}),
    Plot.lineY(series, {x: "year", y: "rate", stroke: "series", tip: true}),
    Plot.dot(series, {x: "year", y: "rate", stroke: "series", tip: true})
  ]
})

The crude rate is per 1,000 residents at the observed age mix; the age-standardised rate applies the WHO World Standard Population (2000–2025) (Ahmad et al. 2001), removing the effect of BC’s ageing population. The grey band marks the COVID-19 period (2020–2024) and the red dashed line the crude rate’s detected change point.

Trajectory and regimes

A crude trend summary, recomputed on every build (data/published/mortality_rates_bc_trend.json):

segments = trend.segments.length
  ? trend.segments
  : [{label: "whole series", start: trend.start, end: trend.end, ...trend.overall}]
Inputs.table(segments, {
  columns: ["label", "start", "end", "slope_per_year", "slope_per_decade"],
  header: {label: "segment", start: "from", end: "to", slope_per_year: "slope/yr", slope_per_decade: "slope/decade"}
})
md`Overall the crude rate changed by **${trend.overall.slope_per_decade.toFixed(2)}** per 1,000
per decade across ${trend.start}–${trend.end}. The best single change
point is **${trend.change_point}**${trend.change_point === 2020 ? " — the onset of the COVID-19 period" : ""}.`

Caveats

  • Crude vs standardised. Two series are published: the crude rate (observed age mix) and a directly age-standardised rate using the WHO World Standard Population (2000–2025) (Ahmad et al. 2001). The crude rate can rise while the standardised rate falls if the population ages — seen here, so read the two together.
  • Change point is exploratory. The single change point and segment slopes are a descriptive summary of a short series, not a formal break-point test; the post-2020 segment is especially short and volatile.
  • Observation window and regimes. The series spans 1991–2024 and crosses the COVID-19 period (2020–2024), marked on the figure.
  • Resolution. Published here at provincial resolution. Age-group and sex breakdowns exist in the source table and can be added without changing the pipeline.
  • Revisions. Statistics Canada may revise recent-year estimates; the published files are regenerated from the source on each build.

Reproduce

PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m sohbc fetch statcan_wds
PYTHONPATH=src python3 -m sohbc build mortality_rates_bc

The tidy dataset is published at data/published/mortality_rates_bc.csv, with the age-standardised series at data/published/mortality_rates_bc_age_standardised.csv (standard population weights in data/reference/who_standard_population.csv).
