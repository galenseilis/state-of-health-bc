Indicator: Life expectancy at birth, British Columbia

Status: second published indicator (milestone M3 in progress). Regenerate with uv run sohbc build life_expectancy_bc.

This indicator is fetched from a catalogued Statistics Canada table, tidied by the same pipeline as the mortality indicator, and published as a versioned dataset plus the views below. Each indicator page states its provenance and caveats (see the roadmap, milestone M3).

Provenance

Field Value
Domain Mortality
Definition Life expectancy at birth (age 0), in years, by sex (both sexes, males, females), resident in British Columbia, from the complete life table (Statistics Canada, n.d.)
Source Statistics Canada, table 13-10-0837-01, Life expectancy and other elements of the complete life table, single-year estimates, Canada, all provinces except Prince Edward Island (Statistics Canada, n.d.)

Coverage 1980–2024 (annual, single-year estimates); observation window shown on each figure
Frequency / refresh annual

Proxy note A proxy for the mortality domain, not the state of health itself (why)

The grey band marks the COVID-19 period (2020–2024); the red dashed lines are the male series’ regime breaks, the blue dotted lines the female series’. Life expectancy is a period measure: it is the average length of life a synthetic cohort would experience if it lived its whole life under the age-specific death rates of that year (Statistics Canada, n.d.).

Trajectory and regimes

Trend summaries, recomputed on every build (data/published/life_expectancy_bc_trend.json, data/published/life_expectancy_bc_by_sex_trend.json):

md`Over ${trend.start}–${trend.end} life expectancy at birth (both sexes)
changed by **${trend.overall.slope_per_decade.toFixed(2)}** years per
decade, and the series is **${trend.stationarity.verdict}**. It carries a
monotone trend (Kendall's tau = ${trend.trend_test.tau.toFixed(2)},
p = ${trend.trend_test.p_value.toFixed(3)}) and departure from a single
straight line (residual-permutation test,
p = ${trend.break_test.p_value.toFixed(3)}). The BIC-selected regimes
break at ${trend.change_points.join(", ")}.`
sexRows = ["Males", "Females"].map(sex => ({
  sex,
  ...bySexTrend.levels[sex]
})).map(d => ({
  sex: d.sex,
  start: d.start,
  end: d.end,
  slope_per_decade: d.overall.slope_per_decade,
  verdict: d.stationarity.verdict,
  change_points: d.change_points.join(", ") || "—",
  change_point: d.change_point ?? "—"
}))
Inputs.table(sexRows, {
  columns: ["sex", "start", "end", "slope_per_decade", "verdict", "change_points", "change_point"],
  header: {sex: "sex", start: "from", end: "to", slope_per_decade: "slope/decade", verdict: "stationarity", change_points: "regime breaks", change_point: "largest break"}
})
md`Both sex-specific series are **${bySexTrend.levels.Males.stationarity.verdict}** over
${bySexTrend.levels.Males.start}–${bySexTrend.levels.Males.end}. Females
live longer than males at every observed year, and the female advantage is
roughly constant in levels though not in the dating of its regimes. The
period includes the COVID-19 mortality shock, visible as the 2020–2022
dip; the pipeline dates it and does not attribute it.`

Method. Regimes are dated by a piecewise-linear segmentation, solved exactly by dynamic programming, with the number of breaks chosen by BIC; whether the series departs from one straight line is tested by residual permutation (Truong et al. 2020), and the trend by Kendall’s tau. Each series is therefore labelled stationary or not over its observed window, as milestone M3 requires. The full test output, including every candidate segmentation, is in the trend JSON. The permutation test assumes exchangeable residuals — autocorrelation is not modelled — so it is a test of departure from a linear trend, not a full time-series model.

Caveats

  • Period, not cohort. Life expectancy at birth is a period life-table quantity: it is not the expected lifespan of anyone actually born in that year, and it says nothing about the health of the living (Statistics Canada, n.d.).
  • Break detection, not explanation. The regimes are dated by a formal test (above); the test says a break beyond a single straight line exists, not what caused it. Recent-year estimates rest on few data points and are the least certain.
  • Sex categories are the source’s. The table publishes both sexes, males, and females; the pipeline reports these as given.
  • Coverage. The source series is 1980–2024 and excludes Prince Edward Island from the published table; the BC series itself is complete. This table has no sub-provincial rows — health-authority and regional life tables are separate Statistics Canada products.
  • Revisions. Statistics Canada may revise recent-year estimates; the published files are regenerated from the source on each build.

Reproduce

uv run sohbc fetch statcan_life_tables
uv run sohbc build life_expectancy_bc

The tidy dataset is published at data/published/life_expectancy_bc.csv (all ages and elements, by sex). The headline trend tests are at data/published/life_expectancy_bc_trend.json and the per-sex tests at data/published/life_expectancy_bc_by_sex_trend.json.
