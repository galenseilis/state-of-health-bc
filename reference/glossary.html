Glossary

Terms are listed alphabetically. This glossary grows with milestone M1 (definition) in the roadmap.

Causal structure
A representation (typically a directed acyclic graph, or DAG) of the assumed cause-and-effect relationships among variables, used to reason about what interventions would change health outcomes (Pearl 2009).
Construct
The thing a measurement is intended to represent — for example the state of health, or one of its domains. Constructs are real but not directly observable; measurements of them are proxies. See the definition of the state of health.
Data
Construed broadly: any publicly available information that can be appropriately used for the project’s goals — from microdata through published summary statistics, indicator tables, dashboards, and report figures.
Definition (provisional)
The project’s working definition of the state of health. It is versioned and expected to evolve as the project learns what is measurable; each revision records its trigger and its changes. See the definition document.
Diátaxis
The documentation framework this site follows, organising docs into four modes: tutorials, how-to guides, reference, and explanation (Procida, n.d.).
Health authority
One of the regional organisations delivering health services in BC (e.g. Vancouver Coastal Health, Fraser Health) (Government of British Columbia, Ministry of Health, n.d.). A common geographic breakdown for BC health data.
Indicator
A defined, sourced, reproducible measurement used to represent some aspect of the state of health (e.g. life expectancy at birth). An indicator is a proxy, never the construct itself.
Measurement framework
The scheme of domains and candidate indicators that operationalises the project’s definition of the state of health. See the measurement framework.
Multilevel model
A statistical model in which data at more than one level of resolution (e.g. individuals within regions) are modelled jointly, allowing information to be shared across levels (Gelman and Hill 2007). One of the ways the project fuses pictures at different resolutions.
Nonstationarity
The property of a series or relationship whose statistical properties (level, variability, association with other variables) change over time (Box et al. 2015; Hyndman and Athanasopoulos 2021). The project assumes BC’s health is nonstationary in all three senses; see Health is not stationary.
Observation window
The time period a reported result was estimated on. Every published figure in this project carries its observation window, because a result true for one window may not hold for another.
Proxy
A measurement used in place of a construct that cannot be observed directly — every indicator in this project is a proxy. Proxies carry measurement error and construct error, and can drift over time; they are never identical with the state of health they track. See Proxies are not the state of health.
Regime shift
An abrupt change in the level or behaviour of a series — e.g. an epidemic, health system reform, or coding definition change — after which the series is effectively a different one. Comparisons spanning a regime shift are treated separately from comparisons within a regime.
Resolution
The level at which a datum or result is observed — individual, neighbourhood, HSDA, health authority, provincial, or external/non-BC. This project uses all available resolutions and records the resolution of every result.
State of health (SoH)
The condition of health in a population at a point in time and space — the central quantity this project describes and predicts. Defined provisionally in the definition document.
Stationarity
The opposite of nonstationarity: a series or relationship whose statistical properties hold steady over the analysis window. Pooled single estimates are only reported when stationarity is supported (Box et al. 2015; Hyndman and Athanasopoulos 2021); see Health is not stationary.
