Indicator: Life expectancy across BC health regions

Status: a published sub-provincial indicator (milestone M3 in progress). Regenerate with uv run sohbc build life_expectancy_bc_regions.

This indicator carries the sub-provincial view beyond mortality: life expectancy at birth and at age 65 for BC’s five health authorities and 16 health service delivery areas, from Statistics Canada table 13-10-0389-01 (Statistics Canada, n.d.). It is the only published sub-provincial life expectancy for British Columbia — but its window ends at 2015/2017 and its boundaries are the 2018 set, so it is deliberately read alongside, not merged with, the newer mortality-inequality indicator (2023 boundaries, 2020–2023 periods).

The regional gap

summary = await FileAttachment("../data/published/life_expectancy_bc_regions_summary.json").json()
rows = await FileAttachment("../data/published/life_expectancy_bc_regions.csv").csv({typed: true})
atBirth = summary.series.find(s => s.age_group === "At birth")
at65 = summary.series.find(s => s.age_group === "At age 65")
latestPeriod = summary.reference_periods[summary.reference_periods.length - 1]
haSpread = atBirth.spread.health_authority[latestPeriod]
short = n => n.replace(" Health Authority", "").replace(" Health Service Delivery Area", "")
haRows = haSpread.regions.map(r => ({region: short(r.region), value: r.value}))
prov = atBirth.trajectories.province["British Columbia"]
Plot.plot({
  title: `Life expectancy at birth by health authority, ${latestPeriod} (years)`,
  marginLeft: 140,
  x: {grid: true, label: "Years"},
  y: {label: null, domain: haRows.map(d => d.region)},
  marks: [
    Plot.barX(haRows, {y: "region", x: "value", fill: "value", tip: true})
  ],
  color: {scheme: "Teals", legend: false}
})

Trajectories

haLines = rows.filter(d =>
  d.age_group === "At birth" && d.sex === "Both sexes" &&
  d.region.endsWith("Health Authority") && d.characteristic === "Life expectancy"
).map(d => ({year: Number(String(d.ref_date).slice(0, 4)), value: d.value, region: short(d.region)}))
Plot.plot({
  title: "Life expectancy at birth by health authority, both sexes",
  y: {grid: true, label: "Years", domain: [77, 86]},
  x: {label: "first year of three-year period"},
  color: {legend: true},
  marks: [
    Plot.line(haLines, {x: "year", y: "value", stroke: "region", tip: true})
  ]
})
trajRows = ["At birth", "At age 65"].flatMap(age => {
  const s = age === "At birth" ? atBirth : at65
  return Object.entries(s.trajectories.health_authority).map(([region, t]) => ({
    age,
    region: short(region),
    slope_per_decade: t.overall.slope_per_decade,
    verdict: t.stationarity.verdict
  }))
})
Inputs.table(trajRows, {
  columns: ["age", "region", "slope_per_decade", "verdict"],
  header: {age: "measure", region: "health authority", slope_per_decade: "slope/decade (years)", verdict: "stationarity"},
  width: {region: 200}
})
birthRange0 = atBirth.spread.health_authority[summary.reference_periods[0]].range
birthRange1 = haSpread.range
at65Spread0 = at65.spread.health_authority[summary.reference_periods[0]]
at65Spread1 = at65.spread.health_authority[latestPeriod]
hsdaLatest = atBirth.spread.health_service_delivery_area[latestPeriod]
md`Every health authority gained life expectancy over the window, but
unequally: the authority gap at birth **widened from ${birthRange0.toFixed(1)}
to ${birthRange1.toFixed(1)} years** across
${summary.reference_periods[0]}–${latestPeriod}, and at age 65 from
${at65Spread0.range.toFixed(1)} to ${at65Spread1.range.toFixed(1)} years.
Northern Health Authority sits lowest throughout. At the finer
service-delivery-area level the ${latestPeriod} gap is
**${hsdaLatest.range.toFixed(1)} years**, from
${short(hsdaLatest.lowest.region)} (${hsdaLatest.lowest.value.toFixed(1)}) to
${short(hsdaLatest.highest.region)} (${hsdaLatest.highest.value.toFixed(1)}).
The provincial trajectory turns at **${prov.change_point}**: a fast rise
before, flat after.`

Caveats

  • Ends before the pandemic. The table stops at 2015/2017, so nothing here reflects COVID-19 mortality or the post-2020 reversal the provincial life-expectancy series carries. It is a baseline, not the current regional picture.
  • Overlapping periods inflate the tests. Each three-year period shares two years with its neighbour, so the ten points are strongly autocorrelated; the permutation break test and the trend test treat them as independent and are correspondingly overpowered. A nonstationary label here means a real rise over the window, not ten independent annual votes.
  • Boundaries differ. This table uses 2018 health-region boundaries; the mortality-inequality indicator uses the 2023 set. The two regional views are not like-for-like and are never pooled.
  • Wide small-area intervals. Every estimate carries a 95% confidence interval, wide at the service-delivery-area level; the ordering of neighbouring areas is soft, the authority-level pattern is not.
  • Period measure. Life expectancy is what a synthetic cohort would live under the period’s death rates; it reports nothing about the health of people then alive, at any resolution.
  • No causal claim. The regional gap co-moves with remoteness, income, and service access; attribution is M4/M5 work.

Reproduce

uv run sohbc fetch statcan_life_tables_regions
uv run sohbc build life_expectancy_bc_regions

The tidy dataset is published at data/published/life_expectancy_bc_regions.csv (all regions, sexes, ages, periods) and the summary — per-period spread and per-region trajectories — at data/published/life_expectancy_bc_regions_summary.json.
