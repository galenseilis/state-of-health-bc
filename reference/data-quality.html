Data quality

Status: v0.1 (milestone M2 in progress). This is the project’s running register of known problems with the data sources. It is expected to grow as sources are ingested in earnest.

What this report covers

The roadmap asks for a register of known gaps, revisions, suppressions, and definitional breaks. Anything that would mislead a reader who trusted a number at face value belongs here.

Known gaps

  • Determinants and stratifiers. Income, education, and employment are used to stratify results (see the measurement framework); BC Stats, the BC Data Catalogue, Statistics Canada, and K-12 reporting are catalogued as their sources. They are determinants, not health outcomes, and are never reported as health on their own.
  • Microdata access. Individual-level sources are catalogued as access routes (Population Data BC; Statistics Canada Research Data Centres) but no approvals are in place yet, so the catalogue is aggregate-only in practice. Nothing individual-level may enter the repository regardless.
  • Neighbourhood-level health indicators. Population denominators exist at LHA/neighbourhood resolution, but health outcomes are not yet catalogued below health-authority level.
  • External drivers. Cross-border patient flows and supply-chain pressures are identified as relevant but have no catalogued source yet; the wildfire, demographic, and macro series are the current stand-ins.
  • Social wellbeing. Measures of social connection and belonging are sparse and carried on a small number of survey series only.

Revisions and provisional figures

  • Substance-use mortality is published provisionally and revised upward as investigations conclude (BC Centre for Disease Control, n.d.). Preliminary and final figures must never be pooled without noting the vintage.
  • Cancer statistics for the most recent years are typically incomplete because of registration lag (BC Cancer, Provincial Health Services Authority, n.d.).
  • Population estimates at sub-provincial levels are revised as administrative sources update; rates computed on them inherit the revision.
  • National survey estimates carry sampling error and are occasionally revised after release (Statistics Canada, n.d.).

Suppression

  • Small-area counts (e.g. vital statistics by small geography) are suppressed for privacy (BC Stats, Government of British Columbia, n.d.). Suppressed cells are missing, not zero; treating them as zero biases rates.

Definitional breaks (regime shifts)

These are candidates for the regime-aware reporting required by milestone M3; each marks a point where a series before and after is effectively different. The pipeline dates regime breaks from the data itself (piecewise-linear segmentation with BIC selection, plus a permutation test for a break beyond a linear trend); the table below records the known causes behind such breaks, which the statistical test can locate but cannot name.

The within-versus-across-regime split in the report is made inferential with a Chow-style F-test of equal slopes either side of each dated break (Chow 1960). It assumes the piecewise-linear form holds and that the residuals are homoscedastic and independent — plausible for smooth annual series, less so for the short pooled survey windows, which is why the test is reported only where a break was actually selected. A significant F means the pace of movement changed at the break, not merely the level.

Break Affected sources Implication
Cause-of-death coding revisions (ICD revisions) Vital Statistics; national mortality Cause-specific mortality series are not directly comparable across the revision boundary
Declared public-health emergency (2016) BCCDC substance-use dashboard; overdose response Surveillance expanded at the emergency; counts before and after reflect different detection intensity
COVID-19 pandemic (2020–) mortality, morbidity, mental health Both a real shock and a measurement shock (care disruption, coding changes)
Survey redesigns (e.g. Canadian Community Health Survey) Statistics Canada health series Break in trend; pre- and post-redesign estimates differ
Indicator-definition updates CIHI indicators Rebased or redefined indicators reset their own series

URL verification

As part of M2, every catalogued URL was checked for reachability with uv run sohbc check --all. All catalogued landing pages and the automated download endpoints returned HTTP 200 at the time of checking (28 sources; the catalogue has since grown to 30 with the two regional survey and life-expectancy tables). The federal publisher’s own portal was unreachable from the verification environment, so federal open data is reached through the Open Government portal record instead; this is noted on that catalogue entry.

First published indicator

The first end-to-end indicator — provincial mortality rates from Statistics Canada table 13-10-0710-01 — is built by the pipeline, including male/female breakdowns and per-sex age standardisation. Its known limitations (provincial resolution only, source sex categories, spans the COVID-19 period, subject to revision) are recorded on the indicator page and are examples of the kind of entry this register is meant to capture. Its regime breaks are dated by the formal tests documented on that page rather than read off by eye.

A second indicator, life expectancy at birth by sex (Statistics Canada table 13-10-0837-01), is now built through the same pipeline; it shares the mortality domain and the same caveat classes (period measure, revision of recent years, COVID-19 period, source sex categories). The source table has no sub-provincial rows.

A third source, Statistics Canada table 13-10-0905-01, supplies a family of survey indicators that widen the framework beyond mortality: life satisfaction and mental health series among them. Survey estimates bring caveat classes the administrative tables did not: sampling error, coefficient-of-variation suppression (flagged cells), and a collection-frame change mid-series, so a step in the series may be a step in the measurement. These are recorded on the indicator pages.

A fourth source takes the project below the province: Statistics Canada table 13-10-0931-01 supplies age-standardised premature and potentially avoidable mortality for BC’s five health authorities and 17 health service delivery areas. It adds two caveat classes of its own — health-region boundaries that change across table vintages, and rates published only as overlapping three-year averages, so the source supports a regional snapshot but not a regional trajectory; the trend machinery is therefore not applied to it.

Sub-provincial coverage then widened to two more sources. Statistics Canada table 13-10-0389-01 gives regional life expectancy for the five authorities and 16 service delivery areas, but only to the 2015/2017 period and on the 2018 boundary set: it is a pre-pandemic baseline on different boundaries from the mortality-inequality view, so the two never pool. It also publishes overlapping three-year periods (ten points sharing two years each), which makes the trend and break tests overpowered — a caveat worth remembering whenever a pooled series is fed to tests that assume independent observations. Statistics Canada table 13-10-0972-01 gives the survey family at service-delivery-area resolution as five pooled two-year periods with heavier suppression than the provincial table; with five points the stationarity verdict is undetermined throughout, cells flagged too unreliable to publish are kept in the tidy file but dropped from summaries, the indicator has a regular healthcare provider is absent, and anxiety disorder is missing from the most recent period.

Governance

  • Licences vary by source (open-government licences to publisher terms). Each catalogue entry records its licence; redistribution of anything committed must respect it.
  • No individual-level data in the repository — a hard rule.
  • First Nations data are governed by the First Nations principles of OCAP® and specific agreements; they are cited for context, not redistributed (First Nations Information Governance Centre, n.d.).
  • “Best use” standard. A source backed only by published summary statistics is admissible when it passes licence and governance checks — the goal is the best use of all publicly available information that can be appropriately used.

Keeping this current

Add an entry here whenever ingesting a source surfaces a problem, and update the catalogue when a gap closes. See How to ingest a source.
