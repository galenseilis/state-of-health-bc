Indicator family: Health survey indicators, British Columbia

Status: a published indicator family (milestone M3 in progress). Regenerate all series with uv run sohbc build.

This page covers the family of British Columbia indicators that the pipeline extracts from a single Statistics Canada survey table, widening the project beyond mortality into physical morbidity, mental health, subjective wellbeing, social wellbeing, and access to care. Each series is published as its own versioned tidy dataset and trend files; the life satisfaction series has its own worked page.

Provenance

Field Value
Domains Physical morbidity, mental health, subjective wellbeing, social wellbeing
Definition Share of British Columbians aged 18 and over reporting each measured characteristic, annual 2015–2025 (Statistics Canada, n.d.-b)
Source Statistics Canada, table 13-10-0905-01, Health indicator statistics, annual estimates (Canadian Community Health Survey) (Statistics Canada, n.d.-b)
Access Statistics Canada Web Data Service (automated full-table download)
Form indicator table (published survey summary statistics)
Geography British Columbia (this table has no sub-provincial rows; the regional view uses table 13-10-0972-01)
Coverage 2015–2025 (annual); observation window shown on each figure
Frequency / refresh annual
Licence Statistics Canada Open Licence
Proxy note Self-reported proxies, not the state of health itself (why)

The family

tLife = await FileAttachment("../data/published/life_satisfaction_bc_trend.json").json()
tPhyH = await FileAttachment("../data/published/perceived_health_fair_poor_bc_trend.json").json()
tMentH = await FileAttachment("../data/published/perceived_mental_health_fair_poor_bc_trend.json").json()
tBelong = await FileAttachment("../data/published/sense_of_belonging_bc_trend.json").json()
tAnx = await FileAttachment("../data/published/anxiety_disorder_bc_trend.json").json()
tMood = await FileAttachment("../data/published/mood_disorder_bc_trend.json").json()
tDiab = await FileAttachment("../data/published/diabetes_bc_trend.json").json()
tBp = await FileAttachment("../data/published/high_blood_pressure_bc_trend.json").json()
tProv = await FileAttachment("../data/published/has_regular_provider_bc_trend.json").json()
tSmoke = await FileAttachment("../data/published/smoker_daily_bc_trend.json").json()
series = [
  {key: "life_satisfaction_bc", label: "Life satisfaction", domain: "Subjective wellbeing", direction: "higher is better", trend: tLife},
  {key: "perceived_health_fair_poor_bc", label: "Perceived health, fair or poor", domain: "Subjective wellbeing", direction: "lower is better", trend: tPhyH},
  {key: "perceived_mental_health_fair_poor_bc", label: "Perceived mental health, fair or poor", domain: "Mental health", direction: "lower is better", trend: tMentH},
  {key: "sense_of_belonging_bc", label: "Sense of belonging to local community", domain: "Social wellbeing", direction: "higher is better", trend: tBelong},
  {key: "anxiety_disorder_bc", label: "Anxiety disorder", domain: "Mental health", direction: "lower is better", trend: tAnx},
  {key: "mood_disorder_bc", label: "Mood disorder", domain: "Mental health", direction: "lower is better", trend: tMood},
  {key: "diabetes_bc", label: "Diabetes", domain: "Physical morbidity", direction: "lower is better", trend: tDiab},
  {key: "high_blood_pressure_bc", label: "High blood pressure", domain: "Physical morbidity", direction: "lower is better", trend: tBp},
  {key: "has_regular_provider_bc", label: "Has a regular healthcare provider", domain: "Access to care", direction: "higher is better", trend: tProv},
  {key: "smoker_daily_bc", label: "Current daily smoker", domain: "Physical morbidity", direction: "lower is better", trend: tSmoke}
]
family = series.map(s => {
  const t = s.trend
  const segs = t.segments.length > 1
    ? t.segments.map(g => `${g.slope_per_decade.toFixed(1)} (${g.start}–${g.end})`).join("; ")
    : "single regime"
  return {
    label: s.label,
    domain: s.domain,
    direction: s.direction,
    slope_per_decade: t.overall.slope_per_decade,
    verdict: t.stationarity.verdict,
    change_point: t.change_point ?? "—",
    segments: segs
  }
})
Inputs.table(family, {
  columns: ["label", "domain", "direction", "slope_per_decade", "verdict", "change_point", "segments"],
  header: {
    label: "series", domain: "domain", direction: "better if…",
    slope_per_decade: "slope/decade (pp)", verdict: "stationarity",
    change_point: "largest break", segments: "regimes (pp/decade)"
  },
  width: {label: 210, segments: 240}
})

Each slope is in percentage points per decade over 2015–2025, for both sexes; a positive slope is not automatically good or bad — the better if… column states the direction that the measurement treats as favourable. stationarity is the pipeline’s label from the formal tests (stationary, nonstationary, or undetermined where the series is too short for the test to have power); largest break is the BIC-selected change point, where one exists.

What the family shows

The series are not all moving the same way, and the direction of movement follows the direction of the measurement:

  • Wellbeing is falling. Life satisfaction (-11.4 pp/decade; break at 2021) and sense of belonging (-10.9 pp/decade; break at
    1. both fell sharply.
  • Poor self-rated state is rising. Perceived mental health fair or poor rose +12.7 pp/decade and perceived health fair or poor +4.3 pp/decade; both are nonstationary (perceived health has no dated break).
  • Mental-health conditions are rising. Anxiety (+6.2 pp/decade) and mood disorders (+3.0 pp/decade) both increased; neither carries a dated break in the current build.
  • Some behaviours improve. Daily smoking fell -4.4 pp/decade, though the regime split at 2021 shows the decline flattening after 2021 (-7.8 before, -4.4 after).
  • Three series are flat. Diabetes (+0.9), high blood pressure (+2.6), and having a regular healthcare provider (-2.1) are labelled stationary over their short windows — no evidence of a trend beyond year-to-year noise.

Read together, the family is a picture of rising self-reported distress and falling life evaluation over a decade, with the clearest breaks coinciding with 2020. The pipeline dates those breaks; this page does not claim they are caused by the pandemic.

The regional view

The same nine measures (all but has a regular healthcare provider, which the table does not carry) are published for BC’s 16 health service delivery areas from Statistics Canada table 13-10-0972-01 (Statistics Canada, n.d.-a), as five pooled two-year periods over the same 2015–2024 window.

reg = await FileAttachment("../data/published/cchs_bc_regions_summary.json").json()
shortRegion = n => n.replace(" Health Service Delivery Area", "")
regRows = reg.series.map(s => {
  const spread = s.spread.health_service_delivery_area
  const period = reg.reference_periods[reg.reference_periods.length - 1]
  const key = spread[period] ? period : Object.keys(spread).sort().pop()
  const d = spread[key]
  return {
    indicator: s.indicator.replace(", satisfied or very satisfied", "").replace(", somewhat strong or very strong", "").replace(", fair or poor", " (fair/poor)"),
    period: key,
    lowest: `${d.lowest.value.toFixed(1)} (${shortRegion(d.lowest.region)})`,
    highest: `${d.highest.value.toFixed(1)} (${shortRegion(d.highest.region)})`,
    ratio: d.ratio,
    range: d.range
  }
})
Inputs.table(regRows, {
  columns: ["indicator", "period", "lowest", "highest", "ratio", "range"],
  header: {indicator: "series", period: "period", lowest: "lowest area", highest: "highest area", ratio: "ratio", range: "range (pp)"},
  width: {indicator: 190, lowest: 170, highest: 170}
})

The regional gap is wide and it does not simply mirror the provincial level: daily smoking varies about 3× across areas, diabetes 2.5×, and mood disorder 2.2×, while life satisfaction — the flattest regional distribution — still spans 9 percentage points. Areas do not rank the same way on every series: Vancouver-metro areas sit lowest on smoking and diabetes but also lowest on sense of belonging.

Each area’s slope over the five pooled periods is published in the summary file, but with five points the stationarity verdict is undetermined for every regional series — the trend test has no power here, and the regional view reports levels and gaps, not dated regimes. Anxiety disorder is absent from the 2023/2024 period entirely, so its regional spread stops at 2021/2022. Cells flagged too unreliable to publish are kept in the tidy file with their status and excluded from the summaries.

Caveats

  • Survey estimate. Every series is self-reported on a national survey, carrying sampling error and a coefficient-of-variation release policy; some cells are suppressed or flagged. Plotted values are published point estimates.
  • Measurement, not only change. The CCHS collection frame changed over the series and 2020–2021 collection was disrupted by the pandemic (Statistics Canada, n.d.-b). A step in a series may be a step in the measurement. Direction of the breaks is dated, not attributed.
  • Short window. Ten annual points is a short series; the stationarity test has limited power, which is why some series are labelled undetermined rather than stationary.
  • Self-report and definition drift. “Percent” mixes reported behaviour, diagnosed conditions, and self-rated state; each has a different relationship to health, and a survey question that changes wording does not change meaninglessly.
  • Provincial table, separate regional product. Table 13-10-0905-01 is provincial only. The regional view comes from a different table (13-10-0972-01) of pooled two-year periods with narrower indicator coverage and heavier suppression — a distinct product, not a disaggregation of the annual series.
  • Revisions. Statistics Canada may revise estimates; the published files are regenerated from the source on each build.

Reproduce

uv run sohbc fetch statcan_health_indicators statcan_health_regions_cchs
uv run sohbc build

Every series publishes data/published/<key>.csv, data/published/<key>_trend.json, and data/published/<key>_by_sex_trend.json. The regional view publishes data/published/cchs_bc_regions.csv and data/published/cchs_bc_regions_summary.json. The selection and definitions are in src/sohbc/indicators.py; add or remove a series there and rebuild.
